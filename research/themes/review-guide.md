# Editorial decision guide

Read the proposition first, then open at least three original passages per theme, including a qualification or contrast where available. Open every passage that will be displayed publicly. Check its preceding and following turns. Verify any attributed quotation against audio, or keep it unattributed. Distinguish the named guest of a conversation from the speaker of a selected passage.

Score each dimension 0 (fails), 1 (revision needed), or 2 (ready). Record episode IDs, passage IDs and a concrete edit wherever a score is below 2.

|Dimension|Question for the reviewer|
|---|---|
|Support|Can you point to an actual mechanism or example in each strong association?|
|Recurrence|Do independent conversations express the idea across different situations?|
|Distinctiveness|Can you say when an episode belongs here and when it belongs under a neighboring theme?|
|Usefulness|Would one suggested assignment yield a specific editorial question?|
|Accuracy|Does the proposition survive the strongest exception in the evidence?|
|Traceability|Do the original words, selected range, source link and displayed label hold up?|
|Public readiness|Can someone unfamiliar with TSSE understand the card and interpret the excerpts fairly?|

Record one of `proposed`, `needs_revision`, `rejected`, or `approved` with reviewer identity, review date, discovery version, evidence snapshot and notes. Reserve approval for an explicit human decision after all seven dimensions are ready. Record decisions separately from regenerable proposals. A score alone never changes status.

## Decisions worth making first

- **Portfolio breadth.** Compare episodes 112 and 197 before treating them as disagreement. One sizes exposure across underlying managers, the other sizes a direct portfolio to leave time and ownership for each founder. Tag the level of allocation in displayed examples.
- **Geographic access.** Episodes 41 and 64 show where money went but say little about how founders gained access. Consider removing these contextual associations, keeping the Kansas City case and organizational bridges. Revisit this theme if it splits into local sourcing and international talent.
- **Insight and demand.** Episode 58 supplies a bridge. Keep both themes if the first answers “How did they learn this?” and the second “What did a customer do or say that tested it?” Merge only if reviewers cannot assign new examples consistently.
- **Pitching and hiring.** Episode 24 links the skills. Use a recruitment outcome to decide whether a passage belongs to hiring. For distribution, prioritize the concrete property-manager and carrier examples over generic partnership language.
- **Voice and omissions.** The archive is rich in investor accounts. Most selected passages have no local speaker label. Check audio before attribution, especially in multi-guest episodes 117 and 149. Sample unmatched episodes from several years with different vocabulary. Review intro, host and handoff language; a passing keyword or repetitive industry term cannot establish recurrence.
- **Source limitations.** Keep industry-specific ideas with only one or two independent examples in a research queue. A narrow evidence set can justify a follow-up interview without a new homepage card.
