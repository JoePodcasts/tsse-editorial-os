Pavel Prata, founder of Murph Capital, shares his unconventional journey from Belarus to Silicon Valley and across the venture ecosystem—from startup fundraising and venture investing to investor relations and allocating capital to emerging fund managers.

Pavel shares the first-principles framework he uses to evaluate emerging managers: understand whether venture is a natural extension of their journey, look for genuine conviction, identify their unique superpower, and keep the strategy simple. He also explains why the best VCs focus on serving founders and how LPs can determine whether an investor adds value beyond capital.

In this episode, you'll learn:

[05:25] How Pavel found his natural advantage by recognizing what he was—and wasn’t—best at in venture.

[10:31] Why an unconventional background can become a feature rather than a disadvantage.

[12:11] Why Pavel started Murph Capital to back the next generation of emerging fund managers.

[18:16] Why the best VCs should focus on serving founders and bringing value beyond capital. 

[24:22] What LPs should understand before investing in emerging managers.

[29:40] How Pavel evaluates fund managers through first-principles thinking, their natural path, and genuine conviction.

[32:40] Why every emerging manager needs a clear superpower—and a strategy simple enough to understand.

About Pavel Prata

Pavel Prata is the founder of Murph Capital. His experience spans startups, venture investing, fund operations, investor relations, and the LP side of venture. Originally from Belarus, Pavel built his career across different parts of the venture ecosystem before launching Murph Capital, where he focuses on the emerging-manager market.

About Murph Capital

Murph Capital is a new media, research, and community platform for emerging venture fund managers and LPs. It combines curated industry insights, original research and open-source data with a community designed to build stronger relationships between emerging managers and capital allocators.
